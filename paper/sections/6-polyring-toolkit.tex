\section{Polynomial Ring Toolkit}
\label{sec:polyring}

The protocol of Section~\ref{sec:iop} is packaged as a reusable toolkit in
the \texttt{emulated} package of gnark~\cite{gnaF}, parameterised by any
emulated field $\Fq$ (via \texttt{FieldParams} type $T$) and any modulus
polynomial $P \in \Fq[x]$. The toolkit operates in a recursive proof
setting where the prover's computations are performed outside the circuit
via \texttt{hints} (Section~\ref{sec:polyring:hints}), while the verifier's
checks are implemented as constraints inside the circuit.

Refer to our fork at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/tiny-gnark-DDCD} for code.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Ring Representation}
\label{sec:polyring:rep}

A ring element is essentially a coefficient slice \texttt{[]*Element[T]} in
ascending degree order. A \texttt{nil} entry is treated as zero by
\texttt{InnerProductNoReduce}---the term is skipped entirely---so sparse
polynomials carry no wasted constraint variables.

The optional \texttt{modEvalFn} of type
$\texttt{EvalFnType[T]} = ([\texttt{*Element[T]}]) \to \texttt{*Element[T]}$
allows handling small-integer coefficients efficiently by multiplying by the small positive
integer first and negating afterwards, rather than multiplying by the
full-width field representative $q - c$.

\subsubsection*{Example: BN254 $\mathbb{F}_{p^{12}}$}
\label{sec:polyring:rep:sparse}

$P_{12}(x) = x^{12} - 18x^6 + 82$ has nine zero coefficients (stored as
\texttt{nil}) and two small non-zero ones. The \texttt{modEvalFn} computes
$P_{12}(\alpha) = \texttt{MulConst}(\alpha^0, 82) + \texttt{Neg}(\texttt{MulConst}(\alpha^6, 18)) + \alpha^{12}$,
keeping intermediate values small instead of multiplying by $q - 18$.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Hint Functions}
\label{sec:polyring:hints}

Hints allow defining computations outside of a circuit. A hint defines an
annotated function in the circuit at compile time. Inputs and outputs are
injected at the solving time. We define these two hints to implement the 
prover's steps in the two-round protocol of Section~\ref{sec:iop:interactive}.

\paragraph{\texttt{polyRingMulHint}} implements \textsc{PolyMulDiv}: it multiplies the input polynomials, divides
by $P$ to obtain $(Q, R)$, and serialises the result as
$\texttt{nbQTerms} \mid Q\text{ limbs} \mid \texttt{nbRemTerms} \mid R\text{ limbs}$.
Inputs are packed as $\texttt{nbBits} \mid \texttt{nbLimbs} \mid \texttt{nbPoly} \mid q \mid \text{inputs} \mid P$,
where each polynomial is prefixed by its term count.

\paragraph{\texttt{quotientsRLCHint}} computes the accumulated quotient
$Q_{\mathrm{acc}} = \sum_{i} z^i \cdot Q_i \bmod q$ coefficient-wise,
corresponding to the prover's step in Algorithm~\ref{alg:2r:prover}.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{API}~\label{sec:polyring:api}

The toolkit exposes three entry points.
\texttt{NewPolyRingCheck} registers a ring group (modulus $P$ plus an optional
small-coefficient evaluator) and returns a handle shared by every multiplication
under that modulus; multiple groups with different moduli may coexist.
\texttt{MulPolyRings} computes $R = (\prod_j A_j) \bmod P$ outside the circuit
via \texttt{polyRingMulHint}, range-checks $R$'s limbs inside, and enqueues the
check $(A_j; R; Q)$ in the group; for longer product chains
a \texttt{PolyRingAccumulator} wrapper automatically triggers intermediate
reductions when the accumulated degree exceeds a configurable bound.
At circuit finalisation, \texttt{performDeferredRingChecks} implements
Equation~\eqref{eq:iop-batched} via two sequential \texttt{api.Commit}
calls~\cite{gnarkcommit} (producing challenges $z$ and $x$) and one
\texttt{AssertIsEqual} per group, so no in-circuit work beyond remainder
range-checks is performed until this point. Full API signatures and a
step-by-step code walkthrough are available in the repository.\footnote{%
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/tiny-gnark-DDCD}}

\subsection{Mapping Algorithms to Implementation}
\label{sec:polyring:mapping}

Table~\ref{tab:alg_mapping} maps each algorithm of Sections~\ref{sec:op-polys}
and~\ref{sec:iop} to its realisation in the toolkit.

The prover's side of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:2r:prover} is driven from the Miller loop of the BN254
pairing implementation (\texttt{sw\_bn254/pairing.go}): each loop step submits
its operands through \texttt{MulPolyRings}, and the resulting quotient--remainder
pairs $(\mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{R})$ are stored in the field object's
\texttt{deferredPolyChecks} queue. The verifier's side
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:2r:verifier} and Equation~\eqref{eq:iop-batched}) runs once,
at circuit finalisation, in \texttt{performDeferredRingChecks}
(\texttt{emulated/field\_polyring.go}). The Fiat-Shamir transformation of
Section~\ref{sec:iop:fs} is applied there via two sequential \texttt{api.Commit}
calls as described in Section~\ref{sec:iop:fs:gnark}, mirroring the two rounds of the interactive protocol:
the first commits the limbs of every queued remainder together with the hinted
pairing witnesses, deriving the RLC challenge $z$; the second commits $z$
together with the limbs of $Q_\text{acc}$ (computed outside the circuit by
\texttt{quotientsRLCHint}), deriving the evaluation point $x$. Absorbing $z$
into the second commitment chains the two rounds, exactly as required by the
non-interactive transcript of Section~\ref{sec:iop:noninteractive}. Both
challenges are native field elements; \texttt{NativeToEmulated} decomposes each
into emulated limbs and asserts the recomposition in-circuit. The
verifier's evaluations are realised by
\texttt{evalPolyWithChallenge}, which shares the cached power vector
$\mathcal{X}$ across all polynomials and accumulates limb products without
intermediate reduction via \texttt{InnerProductNoReduce}
(Section~\ref{sec:polyring:performance}).

\subsection{Usage}
\label{sec:polyring:usage}

\subsubsection*{Core API}

Register the ring group once at initialisation:

\begin{verbatim}
fp, _ := emulated.NewField[emulated.BN254Fp](api)
modPoly := fp.MakePoly(82, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, -18, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1)
ring := fp.NewPolyRingCheck(modPoly, nil)
// Each Miller-loop step then calls:
f, _ = fp.MulPolyRings(ring, f, f, L1, L2, L3)
\end{verbatim}
and \texttt{performDeferredRingChecks} is invoked once by the gnark backend at
finalisation, running the full two-round protocol of Section~\ref{sec:iop}.

\subsubsection*{Accumulate a product chain}

\begin{verbatim}
acc := f.NewPolyRingAccumulator(group, targetDeg)
acc.Mul(A)
acc.Mul(B)
acc.Sqr()       // enqueues (A·B)² without multiplying yet
rem := acc.Eval()
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Implementation Details and Performance}
\label{sec:polyring:performance}

\subsubsection{Schoolbook vs Horner's Method for Polynomial Evaluation}
\label{sec:emulated:flat}

Horner's method for evaluations in \texttt{EvalPoly} across emulated elements causes a linear increase in the limbs size and count per coefficient.
This necessitates reductions after each multiplication.
Instead, we cache powers of the evaluation point $x$ in $\mathcal{X} = (x^0, x^1, \ldots, x^d)$ and evaluate each polynomial as an inner product of $\mathcal{X}$ and the coefficient array $\mathbf{F}$:
$$\mathcal{X} \cdot \mathbf{F} = \mathcal{X}_0 \cdot F_0 + \mathcal{X}_1 \cdot F_1 + \cdots + \mathcal{X}_d \cdot F_d$$

In the emulated field context, Horner's method evaluations are replaced by this inner product with $\mathcal{X}$. This allows us to use the inner product optimization described below.

\subsubsection{Native field operations for inner products}

Modular reductions can be deferred or entirely elided for operations expressible as inner products, such as polynomial evaluations and random linear combinations. This optimization leverages the capacity headroom provided by representing small emulated limbs (e.g., 64-bit limbs) within a significantly larger native field element.

\textbf{Deferred Reduction:} Intermediate scalars are permitted to grow over the integers $\mathbb{Z}$ during limb-wise multiplications and linear accumulations.

\textbf{Preservation of Identity:} Algebraic identities are strictly preserved provided the maximum accumulated limb magnitude remains bounded below the native characteristic $p$.

\textbf{Handling Asymmetry:} In circuits with asymmetric multiplicative depths, operands may accumulate as disparate polynomial multiples of the emulated characteristic $p_{\text{emul}}$. Consequently, a terminal single-element reduction suffices for equivalence testing, bypassing the constraint overhead of intermediate normalization.

\begin{lemma}[Overflow safety for deferred reduction]\label{lem:overflow}
Let $\Fq$ be emulated with limbs of width $b$ bits inside a native field $\mathbb{F}_p$.
Consider evaluating a degree-$d$ polynomial via an inner product over the cached
power vector $\mathcal{X}$.
Since limbs are accumulated independently, each result-limb is a sum of $d+1$
products of two $b$-bit values, so its magnitude is bounded by $2^{\beta}$ with $\beta = 2b + \lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil$.

The deferred single-element reduction is safe whenever $\beta < \log_2 p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[BN254 pairing instantiation]\label{cor:overflow-bn254}
For BN254 pairing verification: $b=64$, $d \leq 256$, and $p \approx 2^{254}$.
Then
\[
  \beta \;=\; 2 \cdot 64 + \lceil\log_2 257\rceil \;=\; 128 + 9 \;=\; 137 \;<\; 254,
\]
so the deferred reduction is safe.
\end{corollary}

Beyond this static bound, the implementation tracks each element's overflow at
compile time and \texttt{InnerProductNoReduce} inserts a reduction whenever an
accumulation would exceed the native capacity, so Lemma~\ref{lem:overflow} is
also enforced dynamically for rings and degrees other than the BN254
instantiation above.
