\section{Introduction}

Blockchain technology is ready for widespread adoption—we have reached a scale where processing Visa-like volumes on-chain is within reach. However, privacy remains a significant barrier to entry for many users and applications. Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a powerful solution to this challenge.

\subsection{Motivation}

Elliptic curve pairings represent fundamental building blocks for numerous cryptographic systems including Groth16\cite{Gro16}, PLONK\cite{plnk19}, , BLS signature schemes\cite{bls05}, and KZG polynomial commitment schemes\cite{kzg10}.

However, \textbf{pairings are computationally intensive}. In execution environments such as blockchain virtual machines, recursive proof systems, and arithmetized circuits, the costs associated with this computation become prohibitive for practical deployment.

\subsection{Our Contribution}

We present a novel optimization for Miller loop computation through a public-coin interactive proof system that verifies individual steps involved in the pairing computation as a polynomial under the Schwartz Zippel Lemma for polynomial identity testing. Over the previous technique employing similar design \cite{FXFM23} our approach achieves:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{50\% reduction} in the pairing computation complexity
\item \textbf{75\% decrease} in auxiliary data overhead
\item Practical deployment enabling ZK proof verification within blockchain transaction limits
\end{itemize}

Our key insight is that instead of optimizing individual field operations, we can treat complete Miller loop iterations as single polynomial relationships, dramatically reducing both computational and communication costs.
