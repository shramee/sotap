\section{Pairing Operations as Polynomials}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:pairing} gives the pairing computation combining El Housni's R1CS optimizations~\cite{PR1CS22} with Novakovic-Eagen's residue witness technique~\cite{OPP24}. The 27th root of unity $W$ scales the Miller loop output $f$ to be a cubic residue (see~\cite{OPP24} §4.3); if $f$ is not already a cubic residue, multiplying by $W^w$, $w\in\{1,2\}$ corrects this.

Our contribution is to express each step of this algorithm as a single multi-operand polynomial product verified via the Schwartz-Zippel lemma, rather than as a sequence of individual binary multiplications as in~\cite{FXFM23}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Optimal Ate pairing with Residue witness check}
\label{alg:pairing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $P \in \G_1, Q \in \G_2, c \in \Fe{12}, w \in \{0,1,2\}$
\Statex $\text{Internal } W, W^2 \in \Fe{12}$ \Comment{$W$ is 27th root of unity}
\Ensure $a_{opt}(Q, P)$
\State Write $s = 6t + 2 = \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} s_i 2^i$ where $s_i \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ and $s_L = 1$
\State $c^{-1} \leftarrow 1 / c$ \Comment{Inverse Residue witness}
\State $f \leftarrow c^{-1}$ \Comment{Initialize with inverse residue witness}

\State $T \leftarrow Q$ \gComm{$T_i$ for each pair for multi-pairing}

\color{gray}
\Statex \small{Every operation involving $T, P \text{ or } Q$ is repeated for $T_i, P_i \text{ and } Q_i$ for multi-pairing.}
\color{black}

\Statex

\For{$i = L - 2$ to $0$}
    \State $f \leftarrow f^2$ \label{pr:ml:sq}
    \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot l_{T,T}(P)$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i, P_i$}  \label{pr:ml:ldbl}
    \State $T \leftarrow [2]T$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i$}
    \If{$s_i = 1$}
        \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot c^{-1}$ \Comment{Residue witness}\label{pr:ml:rw1bit}
        \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot l_{T,Q}(P)$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i, Q_i, P_i$}\label{pr:ml:ladd1bit}
        \State $T \leftarrow T + Q$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i, Q_i$}
    \ElsIf{$s_i = -1$}
        \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot c$ \Comment{Residue witness}\label{pr:ml:rw-1bit}
        \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot l_{T,-Q}(P)$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i, -Q_i, P_i$}\label{pr:ml:ladd-1bit}
        \State $T \leftarrow T - Q$ \gComm{Repeat for $T_i, -Q_i$}
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\Statex
\State $Q_1 \leftarrow \pi_p(Q), Q_2 \leftarrow \pi_p^2(Q)$ \gComm{Repeat for $Q_{1i}, T_i$}
\State $f \leftarrow f \cdot l_{T,Q_1}(P)$ \gComm{Repeat for $Q_{1i}, T_i, P_i$}\label{pr:ml:pi1}
\State $T \leftarrow T + Q_1$  \gComm{Repeat for $Q_{1i}, T_i$}
\State $f \leftarrow f \cdot l_{T,-Q_2(Q)}(P)$ \gComm{Repeat for $Q_i, T_i, P_i$}\label{pr:ml:pi2}
\Statex \Comment{Skip $T \leftarrow T - Q_2(Q)$}

\If{$w = 1$}
    \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot W$ \label{pr:ml:27r}
\ElsIf{$w = 2$}
    \State $f \leftarrow f \cdot W^2$ \label{pr:ml:27r2}
\EndIf

\State $f \leftarrow f \cdot c^{-q} \cdot c^{q^2} \cdot c^{-q^3}$ \Comment{Uses Frobenius maps, Negative exponents use $c^{-1}$}\label{pr:ml:fe}

\Statex

\State \Return $f$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Comparative Analysis~\label{sec:ops:comp-analysis}}

We compare the two schemes for a 3-pair Miller loop zero-bit step (the most common case in BN254's NAF\cite{naf} representation of $s = 6t+2$). Here $F \in \Fe{12}$ is the accumulator, $L_i \in \text{Sparse}_{01379}$ are the three doubling line functions, $R \in \Fe{12}$ is the updated accumulator, and $Q$ is the quotient polynomial.

\subsubsection{Previous scheme}

\textbf{Adapting faster extension field multiplication~\cite{FXFM23}} idea to line \ref{pr:ml:sq} and \ref{pr:ml:ldbl} of Miller loop algorithm \ref{alg:pairing} for a 3--pair Miller loop, we have following polynomial relationships to assert.

\begin{align*}
    F(x) \cdot F(x) &\stackrel{?}{=} Q_1(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) + T_1(x) \\
    T_1(x) \cdot L_1(x) &\stackrel{?}{=} Q_2(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) + T_2(x) \\
    T_2(x) \cdot L_2(x) &\stackrel{?}{=} Q_3(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) + T_3(x) \\
    T_3(x) \cdot L_3(x) &\stackrel{?}{=} Q(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) + R(x)
\end{align*}

$F$, $T_1$, $T_2$, $T_3$, $R$ cost 12C each for evaluation, 4C for $\Fq$ multiplication, and $L_1$, $L_2$, $L_3$ require 4C each for evaluation. All the remainder hints need to be provided for commitment, so this is 12 calldata commitment for $R$ and intermediate remainder polynomials $T_1$, $T_2$, $T_3$. \textbf{Costs sum up to 76C} ($\text{ } 5 * 12C \text{ } + \text{ } 3 * 4C \text{ } + \text{ } 4C \text{ }$) \textbf{and 48 commitments} in $\Fq$ ($4 * 12$). Resulting remainder polynomial $R \in \Fe{12}$ is the result of the Miller loop step.

\subsubsection{Our scheme}\label{sec:op:comp:our}

\textbf{In our approach,} instead of working with individual arithmetic operations, we treat the entire bit operation as a single polynomial. In our scheme line \ref{pr:ml:sq} and \ref{pr:ml:ldbl} of  3--pair Miller loop (Algorithm \ref{alg:pairing}) looks like this.

$$F(x) \cdot F(x) \cdot L_1(x) \cdot L_2(x) \cdot L_3(x) \stackrel{?}{=} Q(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) + R(x)$$

Similar to the previous approach, $F$ and $R$ cost 12C each for evaluation, 4C for $\Fq$ multiplication, and $L_1$, $L_2$, $L_3$ require 4C each for evaluation, but we eliminate intermediate ($T_*$ polynomials above) commitments and evaluations. Only the remainder hint needs commitment, so this is 12 calldata commitment for $R$. \textbf{Costs sum up to 41C} ($\text{ } 2 \times 12\text{C} + 3 \times 4\text{C} + 4\text{C} + 1\text{C (RLC)} $) \textbf{and 12 $\Fq$ commitments}.

\hspace{0cm}

This results in \textbf{46\% fewer constraints} and a \textbf{75\% reduction in commitments}. When verifying ZK proofs on-chain, commitment savings are often more critical than constraint savings, due to the calldata costs associated with transmitting polynomial hints.

\subsubsection{Miller loop: Zero Bit Equation}\label{sec:op:ml-0bit}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:op:0b}
F^2(x) \cdot L_1(x) \cdot L_2(x) \cdot L_3(x) = R(x) + Q(x) \cdot P_{12}(x)

\subsubsection{Final Exponentiation Equation}\label{sec:op:finx}

The final exponentiation check is handled as described in~\cite{OPP24}. The equation handles the cubic scale factor multiplication, and \textbf{Frobenius component} ($q - q^2 + q^3$) of the final exponentation (lines \ref{pr:ml:27r}, \ref{pr:ml:27r2} and \ref{pr:ml:fe} in Algorithm \ref{alg:pairing}). The verification equation looks like:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:op:fx}
F(x) \cdot c^{-q}(x) \cdot c^{q^2}(x) \cdot c^{-q^3}(x) \cdot W^w(x) = 1 + Q(x) \cdot P_{12}(x)
\end{equation}

where $w \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ determines the cubic root of unity scaling factor, and the Frobenius mappings are computed using the residue witness $c$ and its inverse and provided as input. Cubic root is sparse element with just 2 coefficients the degree ranging from 8 to 10 because of positioning of the coefficients. Our $R$ is now $1$ for a successful proof verification.

\subsection{Security}\label{sec:op-sec}

Each verification equation takes the form $P = \prod_i A_i(x) - Q(x) \cdot P_{12}(x) - R(x)$ where $\deg(P) < 2^8$.\footnote{For $k$-pair pairings, $\deg(P) \approx 88 + 18(k-3)$, well below $2^8$ for all practical $k$. This bound can be extended to $2^{10}$ with negligible soundness impact.} By the Schwartz-Zippel lemma (Section~\ref{sec:bg:sz}):

\textbf{Soundness:} $\delta_s \leq d/|\Fq| < 2^8/2^{254} = 2^{-246}$ for BN254.

\textbf{Completeness:} An honest prover always produces $P(x) = 0$ identically, so the verifier always accepts. Euclidean division guarantees unique $Q, R$ with $\deg(R) < \deg(P_{12}) = 12$.
